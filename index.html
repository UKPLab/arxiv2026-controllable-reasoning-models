Abstract

Reasoning traces produced by reasoning models are difficult to control, which can lead to the unintended disclosure of private information even when models are explicitly instructed to avoid it. We propose training models to follow instructions not only in the final answer, but also in reasoning traces, potentially under different constraints. We further introduce a generation strategy that decouples reasoning and answer generation using separate LoRA adapters. To demonstrate this idea, we fine-tune models on a new instruction-following dataset with explicit restrictions on reasoning traces. We evaluate our approach on six models from two model families, ranging from 1.7B to 14B parameters, across two instruction-following benchmarks and two privacy benchmarks. Our method yields substantial improvements, achieving gains of up to 25.5 points in instruction-following performance and up to 50.3 percentage points on privacy benchmarks. These improvements, however, can come at the cost of task utility, due to the trade-off between reasoning performance and instruction-following abilities. Overall, our results show that improving instruction-following behavior in reasoning models can significantly enhance privacy, suggesting a promising direction for the development of future privacy-aware agents.

Method: Staged Decoding

Staged Decoding generates the thinking tokens with one LoRA adapter while the final answer is generated with a different LoRA adapter.

To maximize instruction following performance in both reasoning traces (RTs) and final answers (FAs), we introduce Staged Decoding. This decoding strategy separates the generation process into two stages:

  1. Generating the RT using a LoRA adapter fine-tuned for instruction-following in reasoning traces (IF-RT)
  2. Generating the final answer using LoRA weights optimized for instruction-following in final answers (IF-FA)

This design equips the model with parameters optimized for instruction following in each respective stage. Moreover, Staged Decoding is time-efficient: the overhead of halting generation at the end-of-thinking token, unloading the LoRA weights, loading the new weights, and resuming decoding is negligible in modern LLM inference frameworks.

Key Contributions

  • First training dataset with diverse instructions about how to conduct the reasoning of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) to improve the controllability of reasoning models.
  • Staged Decoding, a decoding strategy that maximizes the instruction following performance of each part of the model generation (reasoning traces and final answers).
  • Empirical demonstration that stronger instruction following abilities improve the privacy of the thinking process of LRMs, with privacy gains of up to 50.3 percentage points.

Results

Instruction-Following Performance

We evaluate instruction-following (IF) performance for both reasoning traces (RTs) and final answers (FAs) on two IF benchmarks: IFEval and Math-IF. Our proposed Staged Decoding consistently achieves the best of both worlds, attaining the highest IF-RT performance and the highest IF-FA performance in 6 out of 10 cases. When considering the average IF score (mean of IF-RT and IF-FA), Staged Decoding outperforms all variants in every setting, with absolute gains over the (untrained) baseline ranging from 1.92 to 25.25 points.

Privacy Improvements

More controllable LRMs, i.e., models with strong instruction-following capabilities in both RTs and FAs, exhibit improved contextual privacy. Staged Decoding achieves the best privacy results in seven of the ten evaluated setups. Specifically, it substantially outperforms the baseline, with average privacy gains of 23.23 points in Password Eval and 22.06 points in PEEP, with a maximum gain of 50.31 points (Qwen 3 14B on Password Eval).

Trade-offs with Task Utility

Consistent with prior work, we observe a trade-off between reasoning performance and instruction-following abilities. Our results show that improved instruction following can reduce utility on complex reasoning tasks, particularly on MathIF. However, on PasswordEval, where utility is closely tied to correctly following instructions, checkpoints with improved instruction-following capabilities consistently achieve higher utility than the baseline.
